# Build the settlement layer Refinery lacks

Nobody has built the full planned design, but the most marketable part of it is already live. **Bittensor SN125 "Refinery" has paid for agent-written LLM-training optimizer code since September 2026, and for data-selection "refiner" programs since October 2026.** Validators re-run each submission on a frozen 360M-parameter setup, with commit-reveal of a source hash and a second confirmation run ([Refinery repo](https://github.com/Barbariandev/Refinery); [refinery125.com](https://refinery125.com/)). That corrects the earlier finding that no project paid crypto for autoresearch-style diffs. A "verifiable autoresearch market" is therefore no longer a novel concept on its own. What no project combines is the rest of the design: stake, a challenge window, slashing for stochastic training claims, royalties paid along causal ancestry, a published hash-committed multi-seed acceptance spec with a measured noise floor, and an open, threat-modelled reference implementation settled on a general-purpose Ethereum L2. The closest design on paper is MAGNET (about 40% overlap, estimated). It has bonds, slashing and notary disputes, but no lineage, and its code is unreleased ([arXiv 2603.25813](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25813)). Seventeen candidate directions were scored. After adjusting for the verified incumbents, an open reference artifact with an autoresearch flagship ranks first. Lean-proof and AlphaEvolve-style tasks score high but work best as cheap test fixtures, because SN66 and ZeroThesis already occupy them. The recommended plan runs about 7–8 months part-time (about 290 hours) and costs an estimated $2.5–8.5k out of pocket, plus an optional $2–10k grant-funded bounty pool. It uses no token, settles on Base Sepolia, targets a preprint around March 2027 and a full paper at NeurIPS or AFT around May 2027, and has a kill gate at every phase. The honest outlook: a publishable artifact is likely, a market that draws outside participants is unlikely, and revenue is essentially nil.

## Refinery runs the market, but nobody settles disputes or pays ancestors

### The live incumbents each hold a different slice of the design

Refinery is the nearest existing version of the planned flagship. Its frozen setup is a Llama-style model of about 360M parameters trained on manifest-pinned FineWeb-Edu with sequence length 2,048 and batch size 32. Compute is capped at one B200 for 20 hours or 188,000 steps, and the objective is lower validation loss. A candidate must beat the frontier by an adaptive bar with a **0.03-nat floor**, a second confirmation run must also qualify, and the frontier is then set from the worse of the two losses. Credit is proportional to the size of the improvement and **decays with a 14-day half-life**. Miners pay evaluation fees in TAO set at GPU rental cost plus 10%. The repo ships a `MINING_AGENTS.MD` that invites LLM research agents ([Refinery repo](https://github.com/Barbariandev/Refinery)). Refinery has no stake, no slashing, no dispute or bisection game, no formal seed-count test and no ancestor royalties: a record built on an earlier frontier optimizer pays that optimizer's author nothing. The repo is small (15 commits, 2 stars), shows no license, and says the source release "is not proof of an active deployment," so mechanism details may diverge from what runs live. Overlap with the planned design is about **55% (estimate)**.

The other live systems are narrower. Gradients SN56 pays only the current tournament's top three (about 76/19/5) plus a non-decaying champion weight. A challenger must clear a one-sided 99% bootstrap with 10,000 resamples on both win rate and mean loss. A three-tier duplicate check ends in a Claude pairwise review of code deltas, fees are burned, and the code is Apache-2.0 ([G.O.D docs/miner.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rayonlabs/G.O.D/main/docs/miner.md)). Teutonic SN3 accepts checkpoint improvements only when a bootstrap lower confidence bound at α = 0.001 clears δ = 0.0015 nats/token. It pays the current king plus up to four previous kings at 20% each, the only live payout that resembles lineage ([Teutonic blog](https://blog.teutonic.ai/)). Teutonic lowered δ from 0.0025 mid-run on 2026-07-21, which shows how ad hoc these thresholds are. Its repo has no license file, so it cannot be forked ([raw LICENSE 404](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/unarbos/teutonic/main/LICENSE)). Templar's Crusades, which paid for `train.py` diffs with timelock commit-reveal and 3-run medians, is probably dormant since Covenant left Bittensor in April 2026 ([one-covenant/crusades](https://github.com/one-covenant/crusades)). SN66 Conjectures.io pays a first-accepted-answer bounty for Lean-kernel-checked proofs of DeepMind formal-conjectures statements, plus partial shares for lemmas. It reports **$95,769 paid to solvers**, with individual bounties of $970–2,230 settled in October 2026 ([conjectures.io](https://conjectures.io)). A deterministic verifier with a first-answer rule needs no dispute game, which is why SN66 has none.

Outside Bittensor the picture splits into two clusters that nobody bridges. The rigorous cluster is centralized and pays cash or nothing. Pluralis's Protocol NanoGPT speedrun (2026-09-29 to 2026-12-01) freezes the harness, requires statistical significance, reruns any entry that beats the record, and offers a **7-day dispute window**, for $18k in cash; its page says "No crypto, no third parties" ([Pluralis](https://pluralis.ai/protocol-nanogpt/)). OpenAI's Parameter Golf requires a 0.005-nat gain at p < 0.01, usually averaged over three runs, and drew 1.4k PRs under an MIT license ([openai/parameter-golf](https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf)). Prime Intellect's frozen `verify.py` accepts a claim only if the mean of 8 fixed-seed runs comes in under 3.27859, but it has no public-submission policy and no rewards ([frontier-automated-speedrun](https://github.com/PrimeIntellect-ai/frontier-automated-speedrun)). The decentralized cluster is weakly verified. Hyperspace AGI's snapshot disclaimer reads "No statistical significance testing", it rewards non-tradeable points, and its archive stalled in April 2026 ([hyperspaceai/agi](https://github.com/hyperspaceai/agi); [rywalker.com](https://rywalker.com/research/hyperspace-agi)). autoresearch-at-home neither re-runs results nor pays rewards ([autoresearch-at-home](https://github.com/mutable-state-inc/autoresearch-at-home)). Numerai is the only mature system in which participants stake on their own claimed performance and get burned. Its marginal-contribution score (MMC) and Merkle-root on-chain settlement are mechanisms to borrow rather than compete with ([Numerai docs](https://docs.numer.ai/numerai-tournament/staking)).

### Three papers and a hobby chain come closest on mechanism

MAGNET (Holo Studio, 2026-03-26) is the most complete paper combining autoresearch with crypto-economics. It specifies commit-reveal with a result-plus-nonce hash, bonds for every node, slashing from 0.1 to 1.0 by severity, notary-checked challenge windows, and epoch reward pools weighted by contribution tier. It **does not address lineage or ancestor rewards**. It verifies by evaluator consensus and a "bootstrap notary quorum" rather than by re-execution. It runs on its own Cosmos-SDK EVM chain, and `holostudio/magnet-core` was still "forthcoming" when the paper appeared ([arXiv 2603.25813](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25813)). The AFT 2025 "Proof of Improvement" paper (PoIm) puts stake, a rollback window and improvement-weighted rewards on an L2. Its setting is toy-scale, though: single-sample updates to a small classifier on a DAO-curated test set, simulated only on Hardhat and Anvil forks, with no code ([arXiv 2510.16024](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16024)). PoLChain is live on Base Sepolia and pays in proportion to improvement over a merged baseline, scored from EZKL/Halo2 proofs of MNIST inference. It has no stake or disputes, its own README admits the challenge pool can be precomputed, and it notes a "paper in preparation" ([polchain](https://github.com/jacksongeiger/polchain)). Its zk path tops out at MNIST inference, which confirms that zkDL-style training proofs are no near-term option: zkDL itself stops at about 10M parameters ([arXiv 2307.16273](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16273); [`Research_papers/2023-1174/2023-1174.md`](../Research_papers/2023-1174/2023-1174.md)).

### Who could finish first, and what has not been checked

The original D-PoDL team is not a scoop risk. The paper by Su, Larangeira and Tanaka appeared at NSS 2023 (LNCS; [ePrint 2023/1059](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1059); [`Research_papers/579857605/579857605.md`](../Research_papers/579857605/579857605.md)). Its few indexed citers ask what useful work could replace hashing: BagChain in IEEE TMC 2025, a 2026 Edinburgh inference-as-PoW preprint ([arXiv 2610.26713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26713)), and IOG's FRLS local-search paper, which keeps IOG on the combinatorial Ofelimos track ([ePrint 2025/2091](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2091)). None builds the market, dispute and lineage stack. No 2024–2026 follow-up by the authors turned up, though dblp and Semantic Scholar author pages were unreachable, so this is an absence of evidence rather than proof.

The realistic fast followers are elsewhere. **Gensyn** holds Verde bisection, the REE runtime, an OP-Stack L2, and now OPEN-1B. OPEN-1B makes every training step of a 1B run bitwise-reproducible across heterogeneous hardware and replayable on one machine ([arXiv 2609.17380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17380)). Gensyn could assemble the dispute layer, though not lineage royalties. **Refinery** could add stake or longer-range credit cheaply, but has announced neither. MAGNET's code release, PoLChain's paper and a "challengeable AI claims" protocol preprint ([arXiv 2609.17631](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17631), title only seen) could all land in the coming months. FC'27 notifications went out on 2026-11-05, and NeurIPS 2026 workshop papers usually post in December. Neither was searched, so a monthly re-check of ePrint, arXiv cs.CR and those lists belongs in the plan. Searches found no 2026 use of the exact phrases "verifiable autoresearch" or "lineage royalties." Google uses "verifiable autonomous research" for citation provenance, so the term needs a crisp definition: re-measured under a frozen multi-seed verifier, with challengeable settlement ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/science-one-framework-a-verifiable-autonomous-research-framework-via-chain-of-evidence/)).

### Five novelty claims survive, and they differ in strength

The first and strongest claim is **recursive lineage royalties with dispute-proof attribution**: royalties flow along the diff DAG to every ancestor a new record includes, weighted by re-measured marginal gain, and attribution can be challenged on chain. Teutonic pays by recency, Refinery by decaying size, SN66 by one-hop lemma shares, and Agora records ancestry but pays nothing. Agora is still the best evidence that the problem is real. In its NVIDIA-affiliated collective-autoresearch experiment, the best method's **145-commit ancestry spans 15 accounts** ([arXiv 2609.18094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18094)). Attribution at scale is still called "an open research challenge" ([arXiv 2503.14519](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14519)).

The second claim is a **published, frozen, hash-committed acceptance spec with a measured noise floor**. It is the cheapest to produce. Each incumbent picks its own bar: worse-of-two runs (Refinery), a 99% bootstrap (Gradients), LCB α = 0.001 (Teutonic), 3-run p < 0.01 (Parameter Golf), and an 8-seed mean (Prime Intellect). Nobody publishes the false-acceptance rate those rules imply. Prime Intellect measured about **54 steps of spread between identical runs** from GPU nondeterminism alone, which is why this matters ([Prime Intellect](https://primeintellect.ai/blog/measuring-autonomous-research)).

The third claim is a **formal security game and cross-system threat model** for training-as-useful-work under rational and agent miners. It would answer the PoUW SoK's objection that utility does not add security ([ePrint 2025/1814](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1814)), a June 2026 paper's statement that such a game is "an open research problem" ([arXiv 2606.24942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24942)), and Pearl's "usefulness gap". On that gap, a 24 EH/s useful-work L1 was shown to produce "zero useful AI computation" because its verifier checks hardness rather than utility ([arXiv 2606.04819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04819)).

The fourth claim is a **chain-agnostic, L2-settled reference implementation** with red-team results. The fifth and weakest is **optimistic bisection to one training step on an L2**, which is mostly engineering on Verde, OPEN-1B and opML. Because the parts are individually citable, a paper will be judged on integration, the security argument and adversarial evaluation, not on new primitives.

One design consequence follows from the cost figures below; it is an inference, not a sourced finding. For a flagship task of 1 GPU × 5 minutes × 8 seeds, re-running the whole claim costs about $0.50–1.80. That is cheaper than any bisection protocol, so the flagship's dispute game should be "a challenger stakes and re-measures under the frozen spec." Bisection to a step earns its complexity only for claims too expensive to re-run, such as a nanochat-class transfer test at about $2,400 per 8-seed verification. Building bisection first would spend scarce hours where they buy the least.

## Seventeen directions scored: cheap verifiers lead, but incumbents shrink every gap

### The 2025–26 breakthroughs that open new directions

Six developments changed what a small team can build. The first is **frozen-verifier autoresearch**. Karpathy's loop found 20 improvements in 700 experiments, worth 11% after transfer ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/17/andrej-karpathy-loop-autonomous-ai-agents-future/)). Prime Intellect then showed agents closing 81.7% of the gap to the human speedrun record under an 8-seed verifier, mostly by stacking known methods ([Prime Intellect](https://primeintellect.ai/blog/measuring-autonomous-research)).

The second is **step-level replay**. Srivastava et al. got exact FP32 replay at GPT-2 scale ([arXiv 2403.09603](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09603)). OPEN-1B extended cross-hardware bitwise replay to 1B parameters, at the cost of int8 GEMMs "several times slower" and incomplete community audit coverage ([arXiv 2609.17380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17380); [pebblous](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/open-1b-auditable-training-run/en/)). OVIG offers tolerance-based replay at +14.3% compute ([arXiv 2606.21045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21045)), and a CEA-LIST proof shows a lazy verifier checking 10% of steps can drive a bounded adversary's success toward zero ([arXiv 2607.06643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06643)).

The third is **near-free verifiers for math**. AlphaProof Nexus solved 9 of 353 open Erdős problems at "a few hundred dollars per solved problem" ([aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/deepmind-alphaproof-nexus-solves-9-erds-problems)), and small open models now set new best-known packing bounds ([arXiv 2511.23473](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2511.23473)).

The fourth is **hardened kernel harnesses**. KernelBench-Verified's hidden input transforms cut the best model from 1.43× to 0.88×, and exposed a ReLU "kernel" that claimed 374× by returning its input ([arXiv 2607.16241](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16241v1)).

The fifth is **cheap rollout checks and consumer RL**. TOPLOC fingerprints inference in about 258 bytes per 32 tokens ([arXiv 2501.16007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16007)), and 14 Macs saturated a B200 trainer with rollouts ([Pluralis](https://pluralis.ai/blog/rl-post-training-on-macs/)).

The sixth is **payment rails that need no token**. The x402 Foundation launched in July 2026 with 40+ members, and AWS AgentCore added x402 support ([agentlux](https://agentlux.ai/blog/the-agent-payments-showdown-x402-vs-ap2-vs-mpp-vs-acp-in-2026), secondary).

The ranking says something sharper than "pick the top row." Verification cost relative to the work separates the directions. Estimated ratios are about 10⁻⁴ for a Lean kernel check, 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁵ for AlphaEvolve evaluators, under 1% for kernels, under 5% for adapter re-evaluation, about 1% of search effort for autoresearch (but k times one run), and 1× or more for data curation, which needs a proxy training run. Those ratios explain why math tasks top every weighting tried and why data and protein markets sink. Incumbents now crowd almost every category. A third-party Bittensor directory lists subnets for kernels (SN10, SN14), distillation (SN97, SN65, SN40), RLVR (SN5), molecules (SN68) and expert add-ons beside a frozen Qwen (SN102) ([doctensor](https://doctensor.github.io/bittensor-docs/), secondary, unverified per subnet). The open niche is therefore not a task category. It is a **protocol layer**: one generic engine (escrow, commit-reveal, re-measure, challenge, settle, lineage) that can host an exact verifier, a noisy GPU verifier and a statistical merge verifier. Re-weighting toward revenue (M×3) lifts kernels, the AI Act notary and the adapter/data-owner market to the top three. That is the fallback if money matters more than publication. Re-weighting toward PoDL identity (F×3) favors adapters, the autoresearch flagship and provenance. Four directions are poor uses of scarce hours: collaborative pretraining, verifiable inference, protein design and hidden-test coding agents. Each pairs a dense incumbent field with hardware or domain demands the team cannot meet.

Two directions deserve note as low-cost spin-offs rather than products. The repo's signed safetensors manifest is one step from compatibility with OpenSSF Model Signing, which NVIDIA uses on all NGC models ([OpenSSF OMS](https://openssf.org/?p=8454)). That step is binding the `files` section into the signature, which the current code omits ([ipfs_utils.py](../dpodl_core/ipfs_utils.py)). The lineage DAG then becomes a provenance registry that OMS lacks. The EU AI Act angle is real but narrow. Commission enforcement of GPAI obligations began 2026-08-02 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)), and "enforcement rests on self-reporting" today ([arXiv 2606.05433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05433)). A training-evidence SDK is the only plausible non-token revenue path, but it does not need a blockchain, and its buyers are few, large and averse to crypto.

## Constraints: miner GPU-days, not gas, decide who can participate

### Costs favour a one-GPU, five-minute task

Chain and storage costs are negligible: testnet gas is free, and a mainnet anchor costs about $0.02–0.04 per transaction ([Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14798701-base-vs-arbitrum-which-l2-should-you-build-on)). Verification is cheap if the task is small. Miner cost is what determines who can play. RunPod's 2026-11-04 Community prices are $0.69/h for an RTX 5090, $2.69/h for an H100 SXM, $3.59/h for an H200 and $5.98/h for a B200 ([RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)). H100 list prices rose between August and October 2026 ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/h100-rental-prices-cloud-comparison)), so budgets should not assume prices fall.

The table carries a competitive point. Compared with Refinery's one-B200-for-up-to-20-hours budget, a one-GPU five-minute task is **roughly 100× cheaper to verify and to enter**. That difference is the main thing a small team can offer participants that a well-funded subnet cannot. A task that needs 8×H200 node-days attracts only labs. The Bittensor route is closed for this team in 2026–27: registration is burned rather than refunded, and the lowest-priced non-immune subnet is pruned after four months. In April 2026, 67 of 128 subnets were burning 99%+ of incentives ([FalconX](https://falconx.io/newsroom/state-of-bittensor-subnet-adoption-trends-network-mechanics-and-covenants-departure)).

### Funding is a patchwork, and EU programs are between calls

No 2026 funder explicitly funds "decentralized verifiable training," so the plan must stack small sources. **EuroHPC AI Factories Fast Lane** grants up to 50,000 GPU-hours for three months, with approval in about four working days, and access is free for AI SMEs including startups ([EuroHPC](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/ai-factories/ai-factories-access-modes/fast-lane-access-ai-factories_en)). That would cover every verification and red-team day in the plan. Whether a French sole trader qualifies, as opposed to an incorporated SAS/SASU, is unverified. Academic users go through the separate AI-for-Science mode. The **Ethereum Foundation ESP** moved to a Wishlist-and-RFP structure in November 2025 but still accepts open applications, runs an annual academic round, and allocated $9.856M in Q1 2026 with a cryptography, ZK and security focus ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/ethereum-foundation-unveils-new-esp-grants-program/); [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/ethereum-foundation-announces-9-85m-q1-2026-ecosystem-funding)). **NLnet/NGI Zero** paused general intake after its 13th call closed on 2026-06-01, to reopen "after summer 2026" under an Open Internet Stack scope. Its current status is unverified ([NLnet](https://nlnet.nl/news/2026/20260601-call.html)). **Optimism Retro Funding**, about 40M OP paid since 2022, rewards shipped work, so it applies only after a public testnet on an OP-Stack chain such as Base ([retrofunding.optimism.io](https://retrofunding.optimism.io/)). In France, the Bourse French Tech Emergence requires a company less than a year old ([les-aides.fr](https://les-aides.fr/cci_creation/aide/VCEv3w/bpifrance/bourse-french-tech-emergence.html)), which argues for incorporating only when the application is ready. Google's TPU Research Cloud is free but requires publishing, and it suits TPU workloads poorly when the recipes are PyTorch-on-GPU ([TRC](https://sites.research.google/trc/about/)). The highest-leverage "funding" is a person: an academic co-author (PhD student or postdoc) unlocks ESP's academic round, EuroHPC's science mode and venue know-how.

### EU and French law are simple only without a token

A no-token testnet keeps the legal surface small. Three payout forms stay outside MiCA: points, valueless test tokens, and USDC or fiat bounties paid as contest prizes. Paying in an existing stablecoin is not issuing a crypto-asset. Any issued token triggers MiCA white-paper duties unless an Art. 4(3) exemption applies, and the exemption fails if recipients must hand over personal data such as KYC, or if the team signals an intent to list ([ESMA MiCA Art. 4](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-4-offers-public-crypto-assets-other)). Since DAC8 took effect on 2026-01-01, payees' exchanges report transfers ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/tax-authorities/1730346/dac8-and-the-eu-framework-for-crypto-asset-tax-transparency)). Sanctions screening of payees is mandatory regardless of amount, which in practice means name and country declarations and geo-exclusion. The US CLARITY Act failed cloture on 2026-09-15, so a US token classification stays unsettled ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/senate-fails-to-advance-the-clarity-act)). The EU AI Act does not reach the flagship. The GPAI indicator is 10²³ training FLOP ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/ja/insights/publications/2025/08/eu-ai-act-news-rules-on-general-purpose-ai-start-applying-guidelines-and-template-for-summary-of-training-data-finalized)), and a 124M model on 1–3B tokens uses roughly 10¹⁸–10¹⁹ FLOP by the standard 6·N·D estimate. French specifics remain unresearched: the legal status of skill contests (jeux-concours), URSSAF/BNC treatment of prize income, and the AMF regime. They warrant one short consultation before the first real payout. The plan budgets for that but does not price it from a source.

Reproduction should be designed from day one to fit about $50 of RTX 5090 or H100 time with pinned containers, because security artifact committees expect modest hardware.

### Coding agents help, but not on escrow logic

For an owner working 8–10 hours a week, three months is about 100–130 hours. The 2026 evidence on agentic-coding speedups is weak. METR's follow-up gives confidence intervals that straddle zero, and METR says its own design can no longer be replicated reliably ([engineered.at](https://engineered.at/articles/metr-s-developer-productivity-research-2026-update), secondary). Agents speed up boilerplate, ports and tests. They should not be trusted on economic logic such as escrow accounting, challenge timing or royalty arithmetic. The mitigations are a small contract surface, Foundry invariant fuzzing, valueless testnet assets, and one paid review before any real value moves.

### Participants come from prestige plus cheap entry, not from emissions

Participation in verified ML contests has two proven sources. One is sustained cash: Numerai pays about $180–530k a month to roughly 600 staked accounts ([Numerai blog](https://blog.numer.ai/numerai-monthly-numercon-agents-staking-risk-1m-nmr-buyback/)). The other is prestige plus a public leaderboard: autoresearch drew 66k+ stars in a month with no prize, and Parameter Golf drew 1.4k PRs. Emissions alone do not guarantee real miners, and token-led agent competitions without durable verified output are failing. Recall Network announced its wind-down on 2026-10-30 ([recall.network](https://recall.network/)). A small team cannot outbid Refinery's emissions. What it can offer is entry on one consumer GPU with no TAO, USDC payouts, lineage royalties that reward building on others, a published and auditable acceptance spec, and paper co-authorship for labs whose agents set records. Success should be judged by **5–10 external submitters and at least one accepted external record**, not by miner counts.

### Licenses limit what can be forked

Several obvious code sources show no license: Prime Intellect's frontier-automated-speedrun ([repo](https://github.com/PrimeIntellect-ai/frontier-automated-speedrun)), Refinery, Teutonic, and, per a June 2026 review, Karpathy's autoresearch ([rywalker.com](https://rywalker.com/research/hyperspace-agi)). Their ideas can be reimplemented, but their code should not be copied. The `verify.py` logic is small enough to write fresh. Permissively licensed components are available: Parameter Golf (MIT), Gradients (Apache-2.0), Templar Gauntlet (MIT), toploc (MIT), PoLChain (MIT) and Distil (MIT). The licenses of modded-nanogpt, nanochat and the OPEN-1B audit harness still need checking before the plan relies on them.

## A seven-month plan with a kill switch at every gate

### Primary path: publish the referee, then open a small market

The recommended primary path is an open, chain-agnostic **commit, re-measure, challenge and settle protocol with lineage royalties**. Its noisy flagship is a one-GPU, five-minute autoresearch speedrun task, and a trivial deterministic packing-style evaluator serves as a contract test fixture. It settles on Base Sepolia with no token, and payouts come from a grant-funded USDC or fiat pool. The protocol is positioned as the neutral reference spec that Refinery, Gradients and Teutonic lack, not as their competitor for miners.

The existing repo contributes concepts and plumbing, not protocol code. Its contracts accept self-reported results: a fresh account can mint the 100-DPDL block reward with fabricated values, and one transaction can halt block production ([ModelRegistry.sol](../blockchain/contracts/ModelRegistry.sol)). Its Ray workers never synchronize gradients ([worker.py](../dpodl_core/worker.py)). What survives is the paper's chain-bound pre-hash, repurposed as beacon-derived seeds and shards for anti-precomputation, its model-referencing graph, repurposed as the lineage DAG, the safetensors-plus-EIP-712 manifest once fixed, the token contract for testnet use, and the dev stack.

### Kill criteria and fallbacks are set before the work starts

The gates in the table encode five standing kill criteria, all estimates of where effort stops paying. The **scoop check** runs monthly: if an incumbent ships stake, challenge and causal lineage together, or Gensyn launches a research market on Verde/REE, the market component stops. The **noise gate** ends Phase 1: if the noise floor makes the target effect undetectable at about 16 seeds, the task is redesigned before any contract work continues. The **replay gate** ends Phase 2. It replaces the earlier criterion of bit-for-bit replay on two GPUs, because OPEN-1B has since shown cross-hardware bitwise replay at 1B scale. The new test is whether re-measurement agrees within calibrated tolerance across RTX 5090 and H100; if not, the project runs per-hardware leaderboards. The **participation gate** applies eight weeks after opening: fewer than 5 external submitters or zero accepted external records. The **time and money gate** triggers if Phase 1 exceeds twice its estimate, or out-of-pocket spend passes about €3k with no grant by the end of Phase 2. Either way the project cuts to the artifact alone.

Four fallbacks follow from those gates. **Fallback A**, used if the project is scooped on market mechanics, publishes the noise-floor and acceptance-spec paper with the cross-system threat model. That contribution survives any incumbent's feature release and can be offered to Refinery or Gradients as a spec. **Fallback B**, for when there are no participants, stops at Phases 0–2 plus a paper. That is still the only open D-PoDL-lineage codebase in which an untrusted submission is measured by someone other than its author. **Fallback C**, for when time collapses, delivers Phase 0, Phase 1 and preprint v1, about 140 hours. **Fallback D**, for when revenue is needed, pivots to the provenance registry and AI Act evidence SDK, which reuse the manifest and Merkle code and need no token.

The real possibility is narrow but solid. A part-time team can be first to publish a calibrated, hash-committed acceptance spec, a lineage-royalty rule that withstands disputes, and an open threat model spanning Refinery, Gradients, Teutonic, MAGNET and PoIm. It can do this for less than the cost of one Prime-Intellect-style agent run. The real constraints are the absence of sponsor demand, an incumbent that already pays the same agent operators, publication windows that make May 2027 the first realistic peer-reviewed target, and an EU legal setting that punishes any move toward a token. The project should not launch a token, register a subnet, chase zk training proofs, require TEEs from miners, or build bisection before the cheap re-measurement game works.

## Conclusion

The market idea was commoditized in about six months. Karpathy's loop appeared in March 2026 and Refinery was paying for agent-written optimizers by September. The protocol underneath it was not. Every live system makes its own unpublished choice of statistical bar, anti-copy check and credit rule, and changes them mid-run. The durable contribution is therefore the referee, not the marketplace. If the plan succeeds, its likeliest impact is a spec and threat model that existing networks adopt, not a network that miners migrate to. That changes how success should be measured: citations and adoption of the acceptance spec count for more than leaderboard traffic.

The cost structure also reorders the original D-PoDL ambitions. When a claim can be re-run for under two dollars, re-measurement beats both proofs and bisection, and the elaborate dispute machinery matters only for expensive claims the flagship deliberately avoids. D-PoDL's two lasting ideas, binding work to chain state and paying the models a result builds on, map onto the two pieces the field still lacks: anti-precomputation seeds and causal royalties. A revival that leads with those two pieces, rather than with consensus or cryptographic proofs, can make a real contribution in 2027.
